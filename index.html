How to read the numbers. “Not to exceed” figures are authorization ceilings — permission to spend up to an amount, not payments. Erie notes: most floor adoptions are voice votes — the minutes print an outcome but no tally, and the cards show exactly that; committee votes are shown where the record prints them; defeated floor items never receive resolution numbers in Erie’s minutes, so they cannot appear as voted leads. Town of Tonawanda notes: the minutes print a status word plus mover and seconder but no per-member tally (verified in the text layer), and the cards show exactly that; bid awards print the winner and the full tabulation — including losing bidders’ prices — so “awarded to” there is the record’s own language; money adopted subject to a permissive referendum shows its 30-day unlock clock; one malformed dollar token in Res 2026-471 (“$10,0000,000.00”) is excluded from every total rather than guessed into a number. Town of Lancaster notes: every resolution prints a named per-member roll call — the drills render all five names, including abstentions, recusals and absences as printed; Lancaster prints no per-resolution “adopted” word, so outcomes are derived from the named tallies (majority of the full five-member board) while the tallies themselves are verbatim; figures in attachments printed after a vote record (legal notices, local-law texts) never count as decision money; the monthly building-permit reaffirmations are tracked as market intel, not shown as leads. Town of West Seneca notes: votes are printed as counts only — the record never attaches a name to a yes or no, so none is invented; meeting #06 (Mar 23) exists only as a scanned image with no text layer — it is quarantined and says so, its decisions are not in this data; the Apr 6 special meeting’s minutes were never posted (agenda only, flagged); money printed as an estimate is labeled ESTIMATE, kept out of every voted-dollar total, and never confused with an award; per-hour wages and warrant fund totals never headline a card.
